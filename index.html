The E‑MTB Evolution

Until recently, David rode acoustic mountain bikes. When his bike was stolen, he took it as a sign that it was time to try something new, and he switched to e‑MTBs. In 2026 he rode more miles and more hours than in any acoustic year, and he climbed far more: 199,759 ft, 59% more than his best acoustic year. And because he no longer comes home exhausted, he now has the energy to train in the gym 3–5 times a week.

01 · The catalyst

The stolen bike and the switch

Before 2026, every season was ridden on acoustic mountain bikes, meaning bikes with no motor. That included more than 12,800 miles on a Specialized SL4 and the rougher backcountry days on a Megatower XL.

By then he was worn out from always working to keep his fitness up, and the climbing had started to feel harder and less fun. What he had always loved was the downhills and exploring.

So when the acoustic setup was stolen, sitting out a season was never the plan. It was time to try something new, and David switched to e-MTBs. The motor handles more of the climbing, which leaves more of the ride for the parts he loves: the descents, exploring trails that had been too far to reach, and more rides with Aksel.

06 · Off the bike

Energy left over for the gym

The e-bike made a difference off the bike too. David now goes to the gym 3–5 times a week, for balance, strength and long-term health. That's the part of training mountain biking alone doesn't cover.

On the acoustic bike, rides left him too tired to keep up any meaningful training off the bike. With the motor doing some of the climbing, he finishes rides with energy to spare, and the gym gets that energy.

07 · Takeaways

What it all means

  1. Far more climbing than ever before. 2026 had far more climbing than any acoustic year: 199,759 ft, 59% more than the old record of 125,296 ft set in 2023. With the motor, steep climbs are no longer what ends a ride, so he can climb big peaks without wearing out.
  2. More riding, not less. 2026 came in at 1,544.5 miles and 181 hours of riding, his most of any year: about 49% more miles and 36% more hours than his 2022–25 averages of about 1,037 miles and 133 hours. Rides got longer too, averaging 14 miles instead of 9–10. The e-bike didn't shorten his rides. It made them longer and steeper, and took him deeper into the Truckee backcountry.
  3. Energy for life off the bike. On the acoustic bike, riding used up all his energy. Now there's enough left for the gym 3–5 times a week, building the balance, strength and long-term health that riding alone doesn't.
  4. A setback that turned into an upgrade. The stolen bike came just as acoustic riding had started to feel like a grind. Switching to e-MTBs put the fun back in climbing, and it led to his best climbing year, regular riding all season, more time deep in the backcountry and a regular gym routine.
